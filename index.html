REBOA CLINICAL DOSSIER & CBME GUIDELINES

AORTIC OCCLUSION ZONES

Zone 1 (Thoracic Aorta)
Depth Estimate: ~40-45 cm (External landmark: Mid-sternum / sternomanubrial notch).
Indications: Non-compressible abdominal exsanguination, ruptured AAA, PEA trauma arrest.
Target: Begin definitive hemorrhage control within 15 minutes when Zone 1 occlusion is used; total occlusion should be minimized, with increased ischemic risk reported beyond 30 minutes.
Zone 2 (Visceral & Renal Paravisceral) - NO-GO Boundaries: Celiac axis to lowest renal artery.
Rule: NEVER INFLATE IN ZONE 2. Balloon expansion can shear visceral trunk origins and causes dense renal and mesenteric infarction without proximal pressure gain advantage.
Zone 3 (Infrarenal Aorta) Boundaries: Lowest renal artery to aortic bifurcation.
Depth Estimate: ~20-25 cm (External landmark: Umbilicus).
Indications: Severe pelvic fracture hemorrhage, refractory junctional groin/femoral hemorrhage.
Target: Less than 30 minutes; maximum acceptable duration is unknown, and total occlusion should not exceed 60 minutes.

INDICATIONS & ABSOLUTE CONTRAINDICATIONS

Primary Indications: Exsanguinating subdiaphragmatic hemorrhage (NCTH) with refractory hemorrhagic shock (SBP < 70-80 mmHg unresponsive to initial resuscitation) or witnessed PEA arrest from exsanguination.

Absolute Contraindications:
  • Suspected Thoracic Aortic Disruption / Aortic Arch injury (Occlusion balloon will cause proximal hydraulic rupture).
  • Pericardial Tamponade (Bypasses indicated ED Thoracotomy).
  • Stable hemodynamic status (SBP > 90 mmHg).

PARTIAL REBOA (pREBOA) & REPERFUSION HYPOTENSION

Balloon deflation can cause ischemia-reperfusion effects and hemodynamic instability. The response varies; titrate only with continuous clinical monitoring and definitive hemorrhage control underway, following the responsible team's protocol.

pREBOA Titration Strategy:
1. Deflate gradually; avoid removing more than 1 mL in a single step.
2. Target a permissive distal pressure (MAP 40-50 mmHg).
3. Administer calcium chloride / bicarbonate & pre-infuse blood products before deflation.
KNOWN COMPLICATIONS
  • Iliac or Femoral artery dissection, thrombosis, and pseudoaneurysm.
  • Lower limb acute compartment syndrome requiring emergency fasciotomy.
  • Spinal cord ischemia and paraplegia (higher risk if Zone 1 occlusion > 45 mins).
  • Aortic wall rupture from balloon over-pressurization.
